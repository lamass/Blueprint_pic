How this chart is built — read this if the "volume bucket" idea is new
  1. Bucketing by volume, not time. A normal candle chart draws one bar per minute regardless of how much traded. Here, a new bucket starts only once ~7,280 contracts have traded (auto-sized to ~50 buckets/day) — so a bucket during a fast, busy stretch closes in seconds, and a bucket during a quiet stretch can take many minutes. The gray "bucket duration" strip in the middle panel makes this directly visible: short bars = fast/active buckets, tall bars = slow/quiet ones.
  2. Scoring each bucket's buy/sell imbalance (BVC). Instead of trying to label every individual trade as a "buy" or "sell", Bulk Volume Classification looks at how much the price moved across the whole bucket, standardizes that move, and splits the bucket's volume fractionally between buy/sell using the normal distribution — a bucket where price jumped up is treated as mostly-buy volume, one where price barely moved as roughly 50/50.
  3. Rolling that into VPIN. VPIN is the average order imbalance over the last 50 buckets, as a fraction of bucket size — a single number from 0 (perfectly balanced flow) to 1 (completely one-sided). The dashed line on the bottom panel marks the top-decile threshold; buckets at or above it are colored red as "extreme".
  4. Did it matter? For every extreme bucket, forward returns/volatility at 5, 15, and 30 minutes were compared against normal buckets. Hover any extreme (red) region to see that specific bucket's actual forward returns.
  5. Turning it into trades. Entry = the first bucket a fresh VPIN crossing goes extreme. Direction is set by the 9-bucket SMA at that moment: price above SMA9 → long, price below → short. Mid-trade conversion: if price crosses back to the other side of SMA9 before the 30-minute hold is up, the position exits right there and immediately flips (short→long or long→short), and that new leg gets its own fresh 30-minute hold. If price never re-crosses, it's a single leg, same as before.
  6. Why SMA9 for direction, not the bucket's own buy/sell imbalance? That was tried first: buy-dominant and sell-dominant extreme buckets both showed positive forward drift, so the imbalance sign alone doesn't predict direction. Short-term trend (SMA9) does better.
  7. Be skeptical of this specific rule's results. Three versions of this strategy have now been tested on the exact same 19 signal events, each refined after seeing how the previous one scored: long-only (+268pt), SMA9-directional (+520pt), now SMA9 stop-and-reverse (+1176pt, 89% win rate). A result that keeps climbing every time the rule is tweaked on the same small sample is the classic signature of curve-fitting to history, not a growing real edge — each version fits this specific 3-month window a little better, which says nothing about live performance. Treat this version as a hypothesis to test on fresh data (a different contract, or ES), not a validated strategy.
