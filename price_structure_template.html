How this chart is built — volume profile & zone detection explained
  1. Candles. Real 5-minute OHLC candles built from every recorded tick (not the volume-buckets used elsewhere in this project) — standard time-based bars, so ranges and wicks read normally.
  2. Volume profile (the sideways histogram on the right). For every candle currently in view, its volume is spread evenly across its own low–high range into fine price bins, then all candles' contributions are summed per bin. This approximates a true tick-by-tick volume profile without needing to ship raw tick data to the browser — accurate enough to see real structure, though a touch smoother than a tick-exact profile. It recomputes every time you pan or zoom, so it always reflects exactly what's on screen.
  3. Zone detection. The profile is smoothed slightly, then local peaks (high-volume nodes — "where price centers") are found. For each peak, the zone extends left/right until volume drops below 45% of that peak's height — those edges are the lines drawn on the price panel. A gap between two zones where volume stays persistently low is a low-volume node, shown as a thin dashed line — the "fast move" areas that tend to get revisited/filled.
  4. Crosshair. A full "+" follows your mouse over the price panel — a horizontal line at the exact price and a vertical line at the exact time, both extending across the whole panel, with the precise values read out in the top-left corner and the stat tile.
