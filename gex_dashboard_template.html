Chart & option chain methodology — controls guide

Candles in America/New York time. Gridlines mark every real strike in view (not a rounded step, so nothing gets skipped), extended 5 strikes past the price range on each side. The 8 strikes with the highest combined call+put open interest for the expiration tab currently selected below — the same totals shown in that table — are the "most active levels": in Tiers mode the top 5 get a fixed full-height color (green = #1, orange = #2–#3, yellow = #4–#5) and everything else falls back to the plain red tiers (three fixed OI thresholds you set below); in Gradient mode every strike outside those 8 is left blank, and each of the 8 instead gets its own small color bump — call color or put color, whichever side leads, picked separately below — confined strictly between the midpoints to its neighboring strikes (so a level's color can never bleed into the next one) with the brightest 20% of that span sitting right on the strike and fading to nothing at its edges — brightness decays by OI rank, so #1 reads brightest. All 8 are labeled C, P, or B on the left for whichever side dominates that strike — B means call and put OI are within 10% of each other, which gets its own solid blue line through the band instead of being forced into a color that would misrepresent a roughly even split (Gradient mode blends the call/put colors for these; Tiers mode's band color is unaffected, since it's ranked by combined OI regardless of side). Hover a C/P/B badge for the exact call/put numbers and split, same as hovering a strike in the table below. The view widens automatically so all 8 are always on screen, even a round, mostly one-sided strike well outside today's price action. Switching the Current day / Next day / Current month tab below re-ranks the chart to match. Drag the price scale on the right edge up/down to zoom in or out on price; drag anywhere else on the chart to pan both time and price (double-click the price scale, or "Reset zoom", to return to auto-fit). "Futures axis" adds each level's futures-equivalent (NQ on QQQ/NDX, ES on SPY/SPX) next to the real price. On QQQ/SPY that's a live ratio against the actual index quote each refresh (QQQ and SPY track their index as a share-price fraction, not a point spread), then the basis you enter below; on NDX/SPX there's no ratio to apply (they already are the index), so it's just the basis. That basis has to be manual: Schwab's API returns no live futures quote at all on this account (NQ/ES quote and options endpoints both 404), so the futures-vs-cash-index premium can't be pulled automatically. It's shared between an ETF and its index — enter it once on QQQ or NDX and it applies to both, same for SPY/SPX — since it's the same real gap either way. Defaults to 0 (pure index-equivalent) until you set it from your own platform; it moves slowly (mainly with rates and days-to-expiration), so it shouldn't need updating often.

A row shows only if it clears both floors below: call GEX or put GEX ≥ the GEX floor, and call OI or put OI ≥ the OI floor. GEX (gamma × open interest × multiplier) is about dealer hedging pressure; OI is the raw contract count behind it — lower either floor for a thin chain (NDX/SPX right now) that would otherwise show no rows. A strike that crosses a floor since your last look is outlined in red (kept only until you switch away and back, or reload) so a newly-significant level doesn't slip by unnoticed. The top 5 by combined OI — same ranking and colors as the chart — get a numbered #1/#2–3/#4–5 badge next to the strike, plus the matching row outline.
